## Part A — Ranked ideas

**Scores are projected submission scores, not winning probabilities.** Breakdown: **Impact /30 + Meta tooling /25 + Demo /25 + Execution /20.** Assumes the scoped version works—not the roadmap.

### 1. Cut Contracts — Client revisions become executable tests

Turn “keep the joke and reaction together; finish on the kiss” into constraints, repair the timeline, and show what passed.

- **Theme / track:** Build for Your World · **Indie**
- **Meta twist:** Spark watches footage and compiles notes into temporal constraints; SAM tracks selected subjects; Python solves timing. Semantic checks remain visibly distinct from deterministic tests.
- **60-second demo:** A revision breaks three requirements. The system repairs only affected spans, renders, and exposes source evidence.
- **Builder edge:** Existing editor, QA pass, real revision history.
- **Risk → cut:** Unbounded creative instructions → five supported revision types, one two-camera sequence.
- **Score: 90 = 26+23+24+17.** A credible leap from AI critique to inspectable action.

### 2. ConsentCut — Make recording consent operational

A producer marks who cannot appear; the system tracks them, proposes alternate shots or masking, and queues uncertain frames for review.

- **Theme / track:** Build for Your World · **Indie**
- **Meta twist:** SAM supplies temporal masks; Spark judges whether masking destroys the shot’s meaning and finds usable alternatives.
- **60-second demo:** Withdraw one participant’s permission. Three deliverables update; a mirror reflection becomes a flagged review item.
- **Builder edge:** Real event footage and firsthand delivery obligations.
- **Risk → cut:** False privacy assurance → no “guaranteed anonymous” claim; one scene, explicit subject selection, mandatory review.
- **Score: 88 = 27+23+22+16.** Concrete stakes and memorable transformation; reliability is the entire product.

### 3. Moment Receipts — Every participant gets a film, not a folder

Produce individual event reels with a coverage ledger showing whose moments were found—and who was missed.

- **Theme / track:** Build for Your World · **Indie**
- **Meta twist:** Spark identifies meaningful interactions; SAM propagates manually selected participants through shots. The system optimizes coverage across people, not just highlight quality.
- **60-second demo:** A beautiful recap omits someone. Select their ledger entry; recover their overlooked reaction and generate a private reel.
- **Builder edge:** Own footage, event clients, existing recap engine.
- **Risk → cut:** Cross-camera identity → manually associate six consenting participants across two cameras.
- **Score: 87 = 26+21+24+16.** Immediately sellable and emotionally legible; distinguish it from ordinary auto-highlights.

### 4. LightLoop — A camera that closes the lighting loop

Given a reference look, measure a physical studio setup, recommend one adjustment, observe its effect, and converge.

- **Theme / track:** **Push the Frontier · Indie**
- **Meta twist:** Spark translates aesthetic intent into measurable targets; SAM isolates face/background regions; image statistics measure changes. A calibration sweep estimates how the light affects each region.
- **60-second demo:** Disturb the light. Three guided adjustments restore the target ratios in a live camera feed.
- **Builder edge:** Studio, cameras, lighting craft.
- **Risk → cut:** Arbitrary lighting inversion → one dimmable light, fixed camera, fixed subject, brightness/background targets only.
- **Score: 86 = 23+24+23+16.** Physical feedback beats another prompt interface.

### 5. ClearPath Rehearsal — Catch event-layout barriers before doors open

Turn a venue walkthrough into a measured review of temporary aisle obstructions and restricted passage widths.

- **Theme / track:** **Push the Frontier · Indie**
- **Meta twist:** SAM segments chairs and equipment; visible calibration markers establish a floor plane; Spark explains measured conflicts and proposes rearrangements.
- **60-second demo:** A cable case narrows an aisle. Move it; the measured route updates from flagged to clear.
- **Builder edge:** Event access, equipment, and a grounded accessibility motivation—not an assumed mobility limitation.
- **Risk → cut:** Unsafe geometric certainty → flat marked floor, static obstacles, explicit uncertainty; no accessibility certification.
- **Score: 84 = 27+22+20+15.** Strong impact, provided measurements—not model guesses—drive findings.

### 6. RoomProof — A listing’s claims should have visual evidence

Audit a property walkthrough against the approved shot list and listing claims before the crew leaves.

- **Theme / track:** Build for Your World · **Indie**
- **Meta twist:** Spark compares listing PDF and video; SAM helps track selected features across views. Every finding distinguishes “supported,” “contradicted,” and “not shown.”
- **60-second demo:** A polished tour misses the advertised balcony. The system identifies the evidence gap and generates a precise pickup list.
- **Builder edge:** Real-estate clients, owned footage, firsthand reshoot costs.
- **Risk → cut:** Invented property facts → assess visual coverage only; never infer dimensions, condition, or code compliance.
- **Score: 82 = 25+20+22+15.** Practical and dependable, but less technically surprising.

### 7. MicMap — Find the usable microphone position before recording

Run a short placement experiment and recommend the position with the best measured speech capture.

- **Theme / track:** **Push the Frontier · Indie**
- **Meta twist:** Spark interprets room imagery and proposes placements; Meta transcription measures consistency against a known script; signal analysis measures noise, clipping, and direct-to-reverberant proxies.
- **60-second demo:** Three labeled positions produce recordings; the winner sounds visibly—and audibly—better in a blind playback.
- **Builder edge:** Studio, lavs, podcast clients, audio judgment.
- **Risk → cut:** Subjective “best sound” → one room, one mic, identical playback source, repeatable measurements.
- **Score: 81 = 24+21+21+15.** An actual experiment, not an audio-advice chatbot.

### 8. ScopeWitness — Turn walkthroughs into evidence-backed scope changes

Compare before/after site walkthroughs and draft a change-order packet containing only supported differences.

- **Theme / track:** Build for Your World · **Indie**
- **Meta twist:** Spark combines video, spoken instructions, and an approved scope PDF; SAM tracks selected assets within each walkthrough. Human-confirmed matches connect visits.
- **60-second demo:** A verbal “move these six fixtures” becomes timestamped evidence and a proposed scope amendment—not an invented invoice.
- **Builder edge:** Agency distribution to small businesses; strong capture and automation skills.
- **Risk → cut:** Weak domain access → one cooperating installer, one staged job; no automated pricing or legal conclusions.
- **Score: 80 = 25+19+21+15.** Commercially credible, but borrowed domain expertise weakens the founder story.

## Part B — The honest bet

### Build **Cut Contracts**, enter **Indie**

You have an unusually strong starting position: a working editing pipeline and a second-eye QA pass. The winning extension is **not another automatic editor**. It is turning critique into a constrained, inspectable repair loop:

**Client note → structured requirements → source evidence → timeline patch → rendered result → checks.**

That is a nine-day project. A general-purpose agentic NLE is not.

**Indie is the better fit under the supplied rules.** Having a production company does not make this new tool production-proven. Startup would invite questions about reliability across codecs, long uploads, queue recovery, collaboration, and paying usage. Those are legitimate questions—and the wrong nine-day workload.

### What a jaded judge remembers on day four

> **“The editor that turns client notes into tests—and shows its work.”**

Not your dashboard. Not your model count. **The moment a vague client instruction becomes a visible, verifiable change to an actual film.**

Use one real revision from your business, with identifying details removed.

**The 60-second sequence:**

1. **0–10s:** Play a polished but wrong cut: the punchline lost its reaction.
2. **10–20s:** Submit: “Keep the joke and reaction together. End on the kiss. Exactly 30 seconds.”
3. **20–35s:** Show the source moments, interpreted requirements, and proposed timeline patch.
4. **35–50s:** Play the repaired sequence. Highlight changed spans—not a complete unexplained regeneration.
5. **50–60s:** Open the evidence: source timecodes, exact duration, final-beat placement, and the semantic check’s confidence/review status.

**Do not label model agreement as proof.** Duration and source ordering can be mechanically checked. “This reaction belongs to this joke” needs evidence and potentially human confirmation. That distinction makes the system more credible, not less impressive.

### Nine-day boundaries

- **One project, two cameras, five revision types.**
- Analyze source footage once; cache structured findings and selected tracks.
- Use short proxy sequences for the live demo; benchmark latency on day one.
- Keep API use comfortably below $150 with a hard spending cap.
- Export a rendered MP4 and your existing timeline format.
- Disclose the pre-existing pipeline; clearly identify the new hackathon work.
- Skip billing, collaboration, arbitrary media ingestion, and a full timeline UI.

**Kill criterion:** If structured instructions cannot reliably change and validate a real timeline by the end of day three, pivot to **RoomProof**. It has a lower ceiling, but a working evidence-backed product beats an ambitious demo held together by narration.
